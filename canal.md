# Modular Canal Infrastructure Proposal
### Sectioned Solar-Canopy Units for Local Power, Water, and Public Safety
**Prepared by: Aaron Hightower — Tower District, Fresno, CA**

---

## 1. The Problem This Solves

Open irrigation canals running through residential neighborhoods currently produce three recurring failures with no accountable owner:

- **Loss of life.** Drownings and recovered bodies occur with disturbing regularity along the same stretches of canal, disproportionately affecting nearby homeowners who are the ones who find them.
- **Attractive nuisance with no mitigation.** Unhoused individuals and people in crisis use canals for bathing because no safe, dignified alternative exists nearby — creating a hazard for them and for residents, with no resources allocated to manage it.
- **Resource framing that excludes the neighborhood.** Canal water and the land beneath/around it is treated purely as agricultural or utility infrastructure, controlled by political and water-rights interests that exclude the people who live alongside it daily.

This proposal reframes the canal not as a single monolithic utility asset, but as a chain of **discrete, governable, instrumented sections** — each capable of generating its own power, performing a locally-assigned function, and being voted on by the people who actually live next to it.

---

## 2. Unit Definition: How Sections Are Measured

- **Base unit width** = canal width at that point (self-referential measure — a canal twice as wide gets proportionally larger units).
- **Minimum unit length** and **maximum unit length** are fixed system-wide (e.g., a floor of ~X canal-widths, a ceiling of ~Y canal-widths — exact multiplier to be set by engineering review).
- **Integral units between bridges**: bridges act as natural hard boundaries. Each bridge-to-bridge run is divided into a whole number of equal-length units — no fractional/orphan sections. This keeps maintenance access, signage, and governance boundaries aligned with existing physical infrastructure (bridges = inspection/access points already in use).

This produces a standardized, repeatable "block" of canal that can be manufactured, permitted, and governed identically regardless of location — like a township grid, but for canal infrastructure.

---

## 3. Infrastructure Layer: Two Standardized Conduits

Each section is built with exactly two standardized conduits running its length, so every unit is interchangeable and serviceable the same way regardless of its assigned function:

1. **Electrical conduit** — carries power generated by that section's solar canopy. Connects to:
   - The local section's own needs first (see Section 4 — local-first priority)
   - A standardized grid-export tap, for any surplus
2. **Plumbing conduit** — carries water for any section assigned a water-dependent function (showers, ice production, cooling stations). Pulls from the canal itself; return/discharge handled per public-health code (no greywater re-entry without treatment).

Standardizing these two conduits means a section can be **re-zoned by vote** without rebuilding the physical infrastructure — only the end-use equipment at that section changes.

---

## 4. Power Use Priority: Local-First, Grid-Second

Unlike a standard solar farm (which exists purely to feed the grid), each section's **first-order obligation is local use**:

1. **Lighting** — canal-adjacent lighting for safety/visibility (directly addresses the drowning/nighttime-incident problem).
2. **Assigned community function** for that section, voted in by the surrounding residents — examples:
   - **Cooling/ice section** — produces ice or chilled water for heat relief (directly relevant in Fresno summers).
   - **Hygiene section** — powers free shower facilities for unhoused individuals, addressing the bathing-in-canal hazard with a dignified, plumbed alternative.
   - **Safety/monitoring section** — powers sensors, emergency call points, and/or rescue equipment (life rings, alert beacons) at known high-incident stretches.
3. **Grid export** — only surplus beyond local need is exported, via the standardized electrical conduit's grid tap.

This ordering is the core of the proposal: **water and power generated *at* a location serve *that* location's people before being sent elsewhere.**

---

## 5. Governance: Vote Per Section

- Each section is a discrete ballot item for residents within a defined radius of that section.
- Residents vote on **which function** a section serves (lighting-only / cooling / hygiene / safety-monitoring / grid-priority), within a menu of pre-approved, engineering-vetted options.
- Sponsors (see below) can propose to fund a specific section's build-out, but the **functional assignment is decided by local vote, not by the sponsor.**
- This is explicitly designed to route around the failure mode where canal/water resource decisions get made for the whole system by political or single-interest actors (agricultural lobbies, utilities, etc.) with no granular, local input. Grassroots, section-by-section voting makes the decision-making unit small enough that it can't easily be captured.

---

## 6. Sponsorship Model: Enumerated Roles Per Section

To fund construction without losing local control of function, each section can have sponsors with clearly enumerated, limited roles:

| Role | Responsibility | Does NOT include |
|---|---|---|
| **Funder** | Capital cost of canopy + conduits for that section | Choosing the section's function |
| **Builder/Installer** | Engineering, construction, code compliance | Ongoing operations |
| **Maintenance** | Panel cleaning, conduit upkeep, ensuring no debris/runoff enters public water supply | Function changes |
| **Monitoring/Alert** | Operating safety sensors, emergency beacons, incident response coordination | Construction |
| **Rapid Response** | Physical emergency response capability (e.g., partnership with fire/water rescue) | Funding |

This is deliberately structured like adopt-a-highway-with-teeth: a sponsor gets naming/credit and possibly a tax incentive, but never gets to override what the neighborhood voted for.

---

## 7. Why This Is Buildable, Not Just Aspirational

- **Project Nexus precedent**: California already proved solar-over-canal works at pilot scale (UC Merced study: 13 GW potential / 63 billion gallons saved annually if scaled statewide). This proposal piggybacks on proven engineering, just re-purposes the *governance and power-priority* model.
- **Liability lever already exists**: repeated deaths at a known hazard can trigger "attractive nuisance" duty-of-care obligations for whoever holds the easement (irrigation district, typically). A documented pattern of incidents is itself leverage to get a pilot section approved — it's not just a moral argument, it's a legal one.
- **Fresno fit**: Central Valley canal density + extreme summer heat + an existing unhoused population near waterways makes this a uniquely strong proof-of-concept location, and you're positioned to propose a literal pilot section across the street from your own house.

---

## 8. Suggested Next Step

A single pilot section — one bridge-to-bridge run, instrumented with lighting + one community function (likely hygiene or safety-monitoring given the documented incident pattern) — proposed directly to the irrigation district or city council as a **low-cost, high-visibility proof of concept**, with the dead-body/incident pattern as the lead justification and the Project Nexus solar precedent as the engineering justification.
